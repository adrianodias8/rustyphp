# 2026-10-01 — worker-mode soak (`bench/worker/soak.sh`)

Dev image in the OrbStack VM (arm64), `wrk -t2 -c16`, 2 workers, RSS of the
`ferro` process from `/proc/<pid>/status` every STEP seconds.

| app | max_requests | duration | requests | req/s | recycles | panics | RSS after warm-up |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| hello | 0 | 60 s | 8,678,491 | 144,398 | 0 | 0 | 76.1 → 80.1 MB, flat from 30 s (77.9–80.1) |
| symfony | 0 | 180 s | 1,575,939 | 8,754 | 0 | 0 | 94.6 → 95.7 MB, band 94–106, no trend |
| symfony | 5000, before the fix | 60 s | 19,786 | 329 | 2 | 19,602 | — (every request after the first recycle panicked) |
| symfony | 5000 | 60 s | 555,267 | 9,239 | 110 | 0 | 95.1 → 102.7 MB |
| symfony | 5000 | 300 s | 2,786,744 | 9,286 | 557 | 0 | peak 113.4 at 90 s, then 106.1–107.6 for the last 4 minutes |

No leak per request in either app: Symfony without recycling ends 1.1 MB above
its first post-warm-up sample after 1.44 M requests (< 1 byte/request, inside
the sampling band). With recycling every 5,000 requests the process settles
~10 MB higher (allocator retention across fresh `Vm`s) and stays there.
Recycling costs nothing measurable in throughput (9,286 vs 8,754 req/s).

The bug the recycling run exposed: the GC classify-walk epoch lived in the
`Vm`, so a fresh `Vm` on the same thread restarted it at 0 while arrays in the
thread's unit cache still carried marks from the previous `Vm`'s walks — the
new walk read them as its own and indexed past its record table
(`vm/mod.rs`, "index out of bounds: the len is 109 but the index is 109").
Any worker restart (after a fatal, or a script that returns) could hit it.
Fixed by making the epoch thread-local.
